    What to try next, without a rewrite

    These are patches to the existing tracker, candidate sampler and governor. None of them requires a new planner or a new transport.

    DWA objective

    Add the progress term

    Your avoider is close to the dynamic window approach: sample reachable velocities, then score clearance, heading and speed. The published DWA objective has a heading term and a velocity term, not only distance. Add lambda * max(0, cruise - along) to the candidate cost. That removes the zero-speed equilibrium that breaks seed 101.

    Fox, Burgard and Thrun, 1997

    Velocity obstacle

    Use the collision cone

    A rock maps to a cone of forbidden velocities in your velocity space. Instead of scoring 216 samples, project the current goal velocity onto the nearest point outside the union of cones. Keep the sampler as a fallback for clutter. This is a drop-in replacement for clearance, not a new guidance stack.

    Fiorini and Shiller, 1998

    Safety filter

    Check the next state, not just now

    Your clearance assumes the new velocity applies instantly. A control barrier function safety filter checks that the next state keeps a positive margin: h(next) >= (1-gamma) h(now). With a 0.11 m/s² plant, a 0.3 m/s dodge takes 2.7 s. Inflate the keep-out by the distance travelled during that ramp.

    Ames et al., 2019

    Zero-effort miss

    First-sighting TTC

    Each radar row already has surface range and range rate. Zero-effort miss is the miss distance if neither vehicle manoeuvres. Use range / closing for time-to-go and range * sin(angle) for the miss. That gives a usable TTC before three polyfit samples exist, which matters at a 4.17 s revisit.

    Palumbo et al., 2010

    Tangent escape

    Never settle on zero

    When every candidate looks unsafe, a tangent-bug style fallback follows the obstacle boundary instead of stopping. In 3D, choose the tangent direction around the keep-out sphere that has the smallest angle to the goal. This is a local-minimum escape, not a global replanner.

    Kamon and Rivlin, 1998

    Reachability

    Reject inevitable states earlier

    An inevitable collision state is a state where no future trajectory avoids collision. Use your actuation limit to reject candidates before the rock is close: if the lateral displacement available before TTC is less than the required miss, the candidate is not safe. This is the same idea as a reaction-time margin, with a hard threshold.

    Fraichard and Asama, 2004

    Keep the direct beeline. For a 1.3 km hop with a local cloud, a global A* or RRT* layer would add latency and fuel without addressing the stop-and-go failure. The fixes above stay inside your current tracker, avoider and governor.
