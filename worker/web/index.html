Technical details 

      

    

    

      Click or focus the canvas to use game controls. H shows or hides controls, held inputs and input settings. F always follows the lander again; Shift+F frames the selected object.
      A fresh Space press launches a paused lander and fires upward thrust while held. P runs or pauses. Space and E use the same reference acceleration limit of 0.5 metres per second squared.
      WASD applies body-frame forces at offset nozzles: W/S forward/back, A/D left/right. E/Q fires reference up/down thrust. Releasing keys stops thrust, not velocity or spin. Ship keys replace the current command with manual nozzle forces.
      H also shows Upright lander, a local test reset, not a competition maneuver. It pauses, aligns the real lander to the launchpad, stops spin and clears thrust without changing position, linear velocity, time, fuel, damage or mission outcome. Each use records a manual intervention so notebook experiments can stop. Release movement keys before flying again.
      Hold Alt, called Option on macOS, with movement keys to move only the debug camera. Shift boosts debug movement only. Left drag orbits or looks, right/middle drag pans, and wheel or pinch adjusts the debug view.
      Keys 1 through 5 select orbit, debug first person, third person, free flight and overview. Keys 6 and 7 show genuine top and bottom ship sensors at 512 by 512 pixels, 8 bits per RGB channel and fixed exposure. Debug controls, HUD metrics, path markers and exhaust cues never modify those pixels.
      The flight HUD shows actual speed/climb in metres per second, terrain-centre clearance in metres and thrust acceleration. Details and Export include force, torque, spin, target range/closing, peaks and estimated fuel/damage. These are local rotating-frame measurements, not STP-equivalent physics.
      Period steps, T changes playback rate, R opens reset confirmation and I opens the inspector. M maximizes; X or Fullscreen enters browser fullscreen. Tab selects game controls and Enter activates them. Space is lift, not button activation. Esc closes panels or releases the view.
      Flight algorithms, predictions and controller scheduling belong in the external Python notebook. The browser runs local physics, sensors and rendering. Notebook pause and step actions affect this local model, not Space Teams Pro. The separate Python adapter calls the real Space Teams Pro SDK only inside its licensed runtime.

    STP's documented success formula
    
      
    Pinned 29 October review. Raw Score = Time Score − Damage Penalty − Fuel Penalty
    Raw Score = 120/T − 2D/209200 − F/1.15
    Final points = 100 × [Raw Score + 0.5]    T is completion time in seconds. D is accumulated collision damage in joules, not collision count. F must be STP's Stats/FuelUsed counter, not this simulator's physical fuel estimate. The local runs cannot supply comparable raw scores or final points. Current STU4-Sim main uses NominalFuel_kg 75.0 and max(F/75.0, 0); see the official competition reference in the README.

    Why no point estimate?
    The published Scoring.py at that pin instead uses max(F/1.15 − 1, 0) for fuel, giving 150 for the nominal run and 165 for the wiki example. Current main uses 75.0 kg and max(F/75.0, 0). The formula disagreement remains unresolved. Both formulas require the official counters. Local SI kilograms cannot be converted from the saved totals. The wiki does not fully specify failure's distance equation. New local runs have no score; historical local totals remain legacy diagnostics, not STP points.
    
      
    
        Why this fuel estimate differs from STP
        
    The local model calculates physical fuel from measured propellant-force impulse divided by 80 s × 9.8 m/s². One archived Tom flight, trial-00004, recorded 66,637.052 N s ÷ 784 m/s = 84.996 kg. That is one flight, not fuel summed across the cohort.
    
        
    The pinned STP actuator source accumulates per-jet acceleration once per actuator iteration. Its timestep multiplication is commented out, and it does not multiply by vehicle mass. STP labels that counter as kg, but it is not this force-based estimate. Links open in a new tab.
    
        
    There is no reliable conversion from these saved totals alone. Exact reconciliation needs matching controller and simulator versions plus actuator history. Recorded measurements stay unchanged, and local fuel is not used to estimate STP points.

      What the counters mean
      
          
      Definition native_actuation_rotation_episodes_v1. A translation burn is a contiguous episode of actual propellant force above 1e-6 N. Angular actuation is actual actuator torque above 1e-6 N m. A physical turn is actual angular speed above 1e-3 rad/s. Counts and durations come from native physics integration, normally every 0.05 s, not controller polls or nozzle pulses.
      
          
      Total orientation angle is the accumulated pre/post-quaternion physical rotation arc, not the commanded angle or the shortest start-to-finish angle. Coasting spin or contact can produce a physical turn without angular actuation. Active flags describe the last integrated interval. Direct local angular torque is not charged to the official fuel allocation; fuel and damage here are local model estimates.
      
          
      Graphs show downsampled public numeric series, at most 192 actual samples per published trial and 48 per current job. Each published repetition remains its own line. Dots are retained observations at actual timestamps. Lines only join adjacent available samples; they do not add observations or establish event boundaries. Missing values break lines and stay n/a, never zero. Axes fit the selected data, not an invented zero baseline. Exact episode totals and longest durations come from the native counters, not the graph. Graphs do not read camera or radar payloads and are not evidence of GPU readiness.
